What’s New for 2025
The following are some of the tax changes for 2025.
Maximum net earnings. The maximum net self-employment earnings subject to the social security part of the
self-employment tax is $176,100 for 2025. There is no
maximum limit on earnings subject to the Medicare part.
Bonus depreciation. A 100% additional first-year depreciation deduction is allowed for certain qualified property
acquired after January 19, 2025. However, you may instead elect to claim 40% (60% for property with a long
production period and certain aircraft) additional first-year
depreciation deduction for certain property placed in service after January 19, 2025. See the Instructions for Form
4562 for more information.
Standard mileage rate. For 2025, the standard mileage
rate for the cost of operating your car, van, pickup, or
panel truck for each mile of business use during 2025 increased to 70 cents a mile.
For more information, see Car and Truck Expenses in
chapter 8.
No tax on qualified overtime. Beginning in 2025, if you
earned qualified overtime pay, you may be eligible to deduct up to $12,500 ($25,000 if married filing jointly) of your
qualified overtime compensation. The deduction is
claimed on Schedule 1-A (Form 1040), not on your
Schedule C (Form 1040). See the Instructions for Schedule 1-A (Form 1040), which are included in the Instructions
for Form 1040, for more information.
No tax on qualified tips. Beginning in 2025, you may be
eligible to take a deduction for qualified tips you received
in 2025. You can’t deduct more than $25,000 of those tips.
The deduction is claimed on Schedule 1-A (Form 1040),
not on your Schedule C (Form 1040). See the Instructions
for Schedule 1-A (Form 1040), which are included in the
Instructions for Form 1040, for more information.
Form 7205, Energy Efficient Commercial Buildings
Deduction. This form and its separate instructions are
used to claim the section 179D deduction for qualifying
energy efficient commercial building expenses that are
now reported on line 27a of Schedule C (Form 1040). P.L.
119-21, commonly known as the One Big Beautiful Bill Act
(OBBBA), section 70507, eliminates the deduction for
new energy efficient commercial buildings for property beginning construction after June 30, 2026. See Form 7205
and its instructions for more information.
Qualified sound recording productions. You may be
eligible to deduct up to $150,000 of qualified sound recording production costs. This applies to costs of qualified
sound recording productions commencing in taxable
years after July 4, 2025, and commencing before January
1, 2026. See section 181 for more information.
Domestic research and experimental expenditures.
Beginning in 2025, you can deduct your domestic research and experimental expenditures as current business expenses or you can elect to capitalize and amortize
the expenditures ratably over a period of 60 months or
more. See Revenue Procedure 2025-28 for more information about making certain retroactive elections under P.L.
119-21, section 70302(f), for domestic research or experimental expenditures.
