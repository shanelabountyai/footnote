Gross nonfarm income of $10,860 or less. The following examples illustrate how to figure net earnings when
gross nonfarm income is $10,860 or less.
Example 1. Net nonfarm profit less than $7,840
and less than 72.189% of gross nonfarm income. You
run a craft business. Your actual net earnings from
self-employment were $800 in 2023 and $900 in 2024.
You meet the test for being self-employed on a regular basis. You have used the nonfarm optional method less than
5 years. Your gross income and net profit in 2025 are as
follows.
Gross nonfarm income . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . $5,400
Net nonfarm profit . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . $1,200
Your actual net earnings for 2025 are $1,108 ($1,200 ×
0.9235). Because your net profit is less than $7,840 and
less than 72.189% of your gross income, you can use the
nonfarm optional method to figure net earnings of $3,600
(2/3 × $5,400). Because these net earnings are higher than
your actual net earnings, you can report net earnings of
$3,600 for 2025.
Example 2. Net nonfarm profit less than $7,840 but
not less than 72.189% of gross nonfarm income. Assume that in Example 1 your gross income is $1,200 and
your net profit is $900. You must use the regular method to
figure your net earnings. You can’t use the nonfarm optional method because your net profit is not less than
72.189% of your gross income.
Example 3. Net loss from a nonfarm business. Assume that in Example 1 you have a net loss of $700. You
can use the nonfarm optional method and report $3,600
(2/3 × $5,400) as your net earnings.
Example 4. Nonfarm net earnings less than $400.
Assume that in Example 1 you have gross income of $525
and a net profit of $175. In this situation, you would not
pay any SE tax under either the regular method or the
nonfarm optional method because your net earnings under both methods are less than $400.
Gross nonfarm income of more than $10,860. The following examples illustrate how to figure net earnings when
gross nonfarm income is more than $10,860.
Example 1. Net nonfarm profit less than $7,840
and less than 72.189% of gross nonfarm income. You
run an appliance repair shop. Your actual net earnings
from self-employment were $10,500 in 2023 and $9,500 in
2024. You meet the test for being self-employed on a regular basis. You have used the nonfarm optional method
less than 5 years. Your gross income and net profit in 2025
are as follows.
Gross nonfarm income . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . $12,000
Net nonfarm profit . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . $1,200
Your actual net earnings for 2025 are $1,108 ($1,200 ×
0.9235). Because your net profit is less than $7,840 and
less than 72.189% of your gross income, you can use the
nonfarm optional method to figure net earnings of $7,240.
Because these net earnings are higher than your actual
net earnings, you can report net earnings of $7,240 for
2025.
Example 2. Net nonfarm profit not less than
$7,840. Assume that in Example 1 your net profit is
$8,900. You must use the regular method. You can’t use
the nonfarm optional method because your net nonfarm
profit is not less than $7,840.
Example 3. Net loss from a nonfarm business. Assume that in Example 1 you have a net loss of $700. You
can use the nonfarm optional method and report $7,240
as your net earnings from self-employment.
Farm Optional Method
Use the farm optional method only for earnings from a
farming business. See Pub. 225 for information about this
method.
Using Both Optional Methods
If you have both farm and nonfarm earnings, you may be
able to use both optional methods to determine your net
earnings from self-employment.
To figure your net earnings using both optional methods, you must do the following.
• Figure your farm and nonfarm net earnings separately
under each method. Don’t combine farm earnings with
nonfarm earnings to figure your net earnings under either method.
• Add the net earnings figured under each method to arrive at your total net earnings from self-employment.
