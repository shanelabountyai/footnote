# Mileage-log template instructions

The firm gives clients a one-page mileage-log template. This memo explains how to hand it out and what each column is for.

## What the IRS requires
Publication 463 says a mileage log needs four things for each business trip: the date, the destination, the business purpose, and the miles driven. The template has one column for each of those, plus an odometer start and end column so the miles can be checked.

## Giving it to a client
- Send the spreadsheet version through the portal, or hand over the paper version at a meeting.
- Tell the client to fill it in the same day as the trip. A log rebuilt from memory at year end is the version that goes wrong.
- The firm does not fill the log in for the client.

## Reviewing a returned log
The bookkeeper checks that no row has an empty column and that the odometer figures add up. The bookkeeper does not decide whether a trip qualifies. That question is for the client and the engagement lead.
